Reproducing the complete report as my final message text.

## 1. Transport protocol — VNC/RFB over TCP, confirmed

- **Screens is a VNC/RFB client, confirmed by Edovia directly.** The App Store product name is literally "Screens 5: VNC Remote Desktop." Edovia's audio FAQ: *"Screens relies on the industry-standard VNC network protocol, which does not include support for sound."* Their marketing page repeats it: *"Screens will not receive any sound from your computer. This is a limitation of the RFB protocol the app is using."* — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/faq/audio-video , https://edovia.com/en/screens
- **No proprietary Edovia wire protocol exists.** Every documented connection path terminates at a standard VNC server (macOS Screen Sharing, or a third-party VNC server on Windows/Linux/RPi). The App Store listing notes Windows PCs *require* a separate VNC server to be installed. — https://apps.apple.com/us/app/screens-5-vnc-remote-desktop/id1663047912
- **TCP only.** Ports documented throughout are **5900 (Screen Sharing/VNC)** and **22 (SSH/Remote Login)** — both TCP. Edovia's guidance: ensure *"the gateway's public port is forwarded to the remote computer's local-network IP address and its port 5900 for Remote Management or port 22 for Remote Login."* Error taxonomy is TCP-socket-shaped: *"Could Not Read From Socket"*, *"Could Not Write To Socket"*, *"Invalid Protocol Handshake"*, *"Already Closed"*. **No UDP, QUIC, or datagram transport appears anywhere in Edovia's documentation.** — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/troubleshooting/stuck-at-connecting , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/errors/vnc-errors
- **SSH tunneling is optional, not the default.** Edovia: *"Screens can establish a secure connection using SSH. This method encapsulates the standard Screen Sharing/VNC connection within an encrypted SSH tunnel."* It is a user-toggled setting ("Use Remote Login (SSH Tunneling)"), requiring Remote Login enabled on the target. — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/how-to/configure-secure-connection , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-connect-4/faq/settings-in-details
- **Screens 5 adds no new transport.** It was a **SwiftUI rewrite of the client UI** plus a business-model change, made universal across iPhone/iPad/Mac/Vision Pro. MacStories' review discusses design and pricing and **does not describe any protocol or networking change**. Released **December 6, 2023**; requires iOS 17 / macOS 14. — https://www.macstories.net/reviews/screens-5-an-updated-design-improved-user-experience-and-new-business-model/ , https://9to5mac.com/2023/12/07/screens-5-mac-iphone-ipad-vnc-remote-desktop/
- **"Screens Express" was renamed "Screens Assist."** It *"grants a trusted party access to a Mac via a unique and temporary link"* and *"configures the router so that the Mac can be accessed remotely"* — i.e. the **same UPnP/NAT-PMP port-forwarding mechanism**, just ad-hoc and temporary, requiring an on-site person to approve the incoming request. *"intended for infrequent use."* — https://blog.edovia.com/index.php/2018/05/07/screens-express-updated/ , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-connect-5/faq/scr-sc-sa

## 2. NAT traversal — UPnP/NAT-PMP/PCP port forwarding + a directory service. No hole punching.

- **Confirmed exactly as suspected.** Edovia: *"Screens Connect requires that your router support NAT-PMP (Network Address Translation - Port Mapping Protocol) or UPnP (Universal Plug and Play), and that the service is enabled and active."* Non-Apple routers must support **UPnP IGDv1**. Confirmed-compatible list is short: Apple AirPort Express/Extreme/Time Capsule (all models), Eero, Google Wifi. — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-connect-5/faq/supported-routers
- **The newer integrated implementation adds PCP.** The Screens 5 error doc lists **"NAT-PMP, PCP, or UPnP"** — Port Control Protocol (RFC 6887) is now attempted alongside the two legacy protocols. Still all router-cooperation protocols, not traversal. — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/errors/port-mapping-errors
- **Edovia's server is a directory/lookup service, not a data path.** The clearest primary-source statement of the architecture, from the Screens Connect guide: Screens *"will determine whether your computer is available on your local network"* and if not, will *"query our Screens Connect server for the computer's network information."* That is a dynamic-DNS-equivalent registry of public IP + mapped port, keyed to a **Screens ID** account. — https://edovia-1.gitbook.io/screens-connect-for-macos/using-your-screens-id
- **The full port-mapping failure taxonomy** (verbatim) shows there is no traversal layer beneath it: *"No router was found on your network"*; *"Your router supports automatic setup, but port mapping is currently disabled"*; *"Your router does not support automatic port mapping"*; *"Your network appears to be using more than one router"*; *"The router rejected the port mapping request"*; *"A firewall prevented communication with your router"*; and tellingly **"NAT Traversal Unavailable" — "The network does not support automatic remote access setup."** When the router won't cooperate, the product simply fails. — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/errors/port-mapping-errors
- **CGNAT is a hard failure with no in-product fallback.** Edovia's dedicated CGNAT article states CGN *"interrupts what should be end-to-end connections, such as those established by Screens when connecting to a remote computer"*, with symptoms of timeouts and a public IP in **100.64.0.0–100.127.255.255**. Their recommended fix is **Tailscale** — *"a versatile, free-for-personal-use solution compatible with various major platforms, usable either in conjunction with Screens Connect or independently"* — or asking the ISP for a static IP. The "stuck at Connecting" doc repeats that CGNAT *"prevents Screens from establishing direct, two-way connections."* — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-connect-5/troubleshooting/cgn , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/troubleshooting/stuck-at-connecting
- **Double NAT also breaks it**, requiring manual remediation: bridge mode (*"Bridged mode disables a router's NAT and DHCP services"*), PPPoE on the router WAN, DMZ, or manually forwarding 5900/22 through the modem to the router's WAN address. Edovia: *"Make sure that you're not using a double NAT network configuration (where more than one router is providing Network Address Translation between your Mac and the Internet)"* — it *"may prevent other computers from discovering or connecting to your computer."* — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-connect-5/troubleshooting/double-nat , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-connect-5/faq/supported-routers
- **Manual port forwarding is the documented first-line fallback**, plus dynamic DNS. The "Alternative Method" doc prescribes: No-IP dynamic DNS + static local IP + manual router port-forward to 5900 — used when *"Your router does not support UPnP or NAT-PMP."* — https://edovia-1.gitbook.io/screens-connect-for-macos/alternative-method
- **Symmetric NAT / STUN / ICE / hole punching: not found.** No Edovia document, blog post, or release note mentions STUN, TURN, ICE, hole punching, or peer-to-peer NAT traversal. (inferred) Since the mechanism is an explicit inbound port mapping to a registered public IP:port, NAT *type* is irrelevant — either the router grants the mapping and there's a public listener, or the connection cannot be made at all.
- **Managed/corporate networks fail for the same reason**, stated bluntly: such networks *"may not support consumer-friendly services like UPnP...and NAT-PMP"*, and *"If you don't have administrative access to the router and port forwarding isn't configured, Screens won't be able to establish a connection with the target computer."* — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/troubleshooting/managed-networks

## 3. Relay fallback — **there is none.** This is the headline finding.

- **No relay exists in any Screens or Screens Connect version, as of Sept 2026.** I checked the Screens 5 guide (~100 articles), the full Screens Connect 5 guide, the Screens Connect macOS GitBook (all 20 pages via its `llms.txt` index), the Edovia blog index, release notes, App Store listing, and the marketing pages. **Relay, TURN, proxy, and "through our servers" appear nowhere.** — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/ , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-connect-5/ , https://edovia-1.gitbook.io/screens-connect-for-macos/llms.txt
- **The strongest negative evidence:** the CGNAT article — precisely the scenario a relay exists to solve — recommends **a third-party product (Tailscale)** rather than any Edovia fallback. A vendor with a relay would sell it there. — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-connect-5/troubleshooting/cgn
- **The relay role is outsourced to third-party overlay VPNs.** Screens 5 ships first-class integrations for **Tailscale** (which itself has DERP relays) and **NordVPN Meshnet**, each with a dedicated section in the connection sidebar. Edovia's framing: *"Screens Connect is the easiest way to access your computer from outside your home or office. But if Screens Connect isn't available or doesn't work in your setup, there's another reliable option: Tailscale."* Screens does no tunneling itself — *"Screens simply connects to the IP address and port provided."* — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/connecting-anywhere/tailscale
- **Consequently: regions, bandwidth limits, self-hosting — all not applicable** (no relay to run). Tailscale's own DERP infrastructure supplies those properties when used, and Tailscale is self-hostable/free-for-personal-use, but that is Tailscale's product, not Edovia's.
- **Screens Connect itself is free and is NOT the subscription.** Edovia: *"a free utility complementary to Screens"* / *"a complimentary service that works seamlessly with Screens."* — https://edovia.com/en/screens-connect/ , https://screensconnect.com/en/index.html
- **Screens 5 did move to subscription — confirmed.** Current App Store pricing: **$3.99/month, $29.49–$29.99/year, $179.99 lifetime** (a "Lifetime Family Upgrade" at $0.00 also listed). Edovia's own pricing page: *"Annual Subscription: $29.99/year; Monthly Subscription: $3.99/month; Lifetime (one-time purchase): $179.99"*, with *"Prices are in USD, may vary by country, and are subject to change at any time."* **Note the price escalation** since launch — MacStories reported launch pricing of *"$2.99/month or $24.99/year"* plus *"a lifetime one-time purchase option that costs $74"*, and 9to5Mac reported $24.99/yr, $3.99/mo, $139.99 lifetime. **Lifetime has gone $74 → $139.99 → $179.99.** Screens 4 owners got *"an exclusive 50% discount on the first year of an annual subscription."* — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/faq/s5-pricing , https://apps.apple.com/us/app/screens-5-vnc-remote-desktop/id1663047912 , https://www.macstories.net/reviews/screens-5-an-updated-design-improved-user-experience-and-new-business-model/ , https://9to5mac.com/2023/12/07/screens-5-mac-iphone-ipad-vnc-remote-desktop/ , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/faq/s5-upgrade

## 4. End-to-end encryption — marketing overclaims; real crypto is optional SSH

- **Marketing claim (verbatim):** *"Screens prioritizes your privacy with strong, end-to-end encryption, ensuring your remote sessions remain secure and private."* And: *"With built-in SSH tunneling, your connection is fully encrypted, safeguarding your data from unauthorized access—even on untrusted networks."* — https://edovia.com/en/screens
- **The support doc is far weaker than the marketing.** The entire "Is Screens secure?" article amounts to: *"Screens uses encryption"* and *"Screens can connect to remote computers through encrypted SSH tunnels."* Note **"can"**, not "does". **No TLS, no RFB security-type detail, no cipher list, no key-exchange description, no protocol version — none of it is published.** — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/faq/is-screens-secure
- **(inferred) The "end-to-end encryption" claim is conditional on the user enabling SSH.** Plain VNC/RFB to macOS Screen Sharing without the SSH toggle is not end-to-end encrypted in the modern sense — RFB's native auth is a weak DES-based challenge-response and the pixel stream is cleartext. Edovia's own settings expose SSH as an opt-in checkbox ("Use Remote Login (SSH Tunneling)" — *"Enable this setting if you wish to have a secure network connection between Screens and your Mac"*), which would be nonsensical if encryption were unconditional. — https://edovia-1.gitbook.io/screens-connect-for-macos/settings/general-settings
- **Does the relay see plaintext? Not applicable — there is no relay.** Edovia's servers carry only account/directory metadata (which computer, which public IP, which port). Session pixels and input travel **directly** from client to the target's public IP:port. (inferred, but strongly supported by the "query our Screens Connect server for the computer's network information" architecture statement.) — https://edovia-1.gitbook.io/screens-connect-for-macos/using-your-screens-id
- **SSH implementation details that are published:** OpenSSH 9 support and modern key formats (**EcDSA, Ed25519**) added in Screens 4.12.3/4.12.4 (Oct 2022); SSH key import fixes in 4.12.17; SSH username rejection bug fixed in 5.8.5 (*"Fixed SSH usernames being rejected incorrectly"*); a dedicated "SSH Keys" feature and "Connecting to Your Mac via SSH Keys" guide in Screens 5. A "Crypto Error" is enumerated in the VNC error list. — https://releasenotes.edovia.com/screens-for-ios , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/faq/release-notes , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/features/ssh-keys , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/errors/vnc-errors
- **Security incident context:** Edovia publishes a "ConnectWise breach clarification" FAQ (distancing themselves from ScreenConnect, an unrelated product) and a blog post **"Important macOS Security Update for Screens Users" (Aug 17, 2026, CVE-2026-65400)**. — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-connect-5/ , https://blog.edovia.com/en/CVE-2026-65400

## 5. Session resumption across network switches — **no session survival; reconnect only**

- **No published claim of session resumption or connection migration exists.** No Edovia statement that a session survives a WiFi→LTE switch. Nothing about MPTCP, connection migration, QUIC, or session tokens. **Not found.**
- **What they actually shipped is auto-*re*connect, not resumption.** Screens 5.6.1 (verbatim): *"If the connection drops or the remote Mac ends the session, Screens will now attempt to reconnect automatically."* Screens 5.7.2: *"Improved detection of closed connections when the app runs in background."* Screens 5.8.10 (July 3, 2026): *"Improved detection of interrupted connections for faster, more reliable reconnections."* — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/faq/release-notes
- **(inferred) The session does not survive an interface change.** The transport is a plain TCP socket to a fixed public IP:port; changing the client's source address invalidates the 4-tuple. The repeated investment in *detecting* dead connections *faster* and reconnecting is the engineering signature of a stack that cannot migrate — you re-dial and re-handshake RFB, losing session state.
- **Caution on one claim:** a first summarization pass of edovia.com suggested "network switching capability for seamless transitions", but a targeted verbatim re-fetch of the same page found **no such sentence**. Treat any "seamless network switching" marketing claim as **not found / unsubstantiated**. — https://edovia.com/en/screens
- **Historical instability in this area:** iOS 4.9.10 (Jan 2020) — *"Fixed an issue where Screens could go into an infinite reconnection loop"*; 4.3.1 — *"Fixed reconnection issues."* — https://releasenotes.edovia.com/screens-for-ios
- Screens 5 has **"On-Disconnect Actions"** as a documented feature, i.e. disconnection is treated as a first-class, expected event with user-configurable handling. — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/features/on-disconnect-actions

## 6. iOS VPN profile / NetworkExtension — **no**

- **Screens does not ship its own VPN or NetworkExtension.** Edovia: *"Using a VPN with Screens 5 is a great way to secure your VNC connections"* — the user supplies it. Explicit disclaimer: *"We cannot guarantee compatibility with all VPN providers, and if you encounter issues, we recommend reaching out to your VPN provider for support."* — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/how-to/using-a-vpn
- **The Tailscale integration is read-only metadata, not a tunnel.** Screens reads the device list from the user's Tailscale account **via OAuth credentials**, then *"Screens simply connects to the IP address and port provided."* The tunnel is owned entirely by the separately-installed Tailscale app and its own NetworkExtension. A known issue exists: **"Tailscale is not detected on the source device."** — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/connecting-anywhere/tailscale , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/troubleshooting/tailscale-not-detected
- **App Store permissions** list only Touch ID/Face ID/Apple Watch protection, encrypted password storage, and SSH keys — **no VPN/NetworkExtension entitlement is advertised.** — https://apps.apple.com/us/app/screens-5-vnc-remote-desktop/id1663047912
- Edovia also notes many VPNs *hurt* rather than help: *"Many VPNs prevent Screens from establishing a direct, two-way connection with other computers."* — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/troubleshooting/stuck-at-connecting

## 7. Latency / compression / adaptive quality

- **No published latency, throughput, FPS, or reliability numbers whatsoever.** All performance claims are qualitative. **Not found.**
- **Only two encodings are named by Edovia: "Progressive JPEG" (as an analogy) and "TightVNC."** Verbatim: *"Adaptive Quality is similar to the Progressive JPEG image file format, in which data is compressed in multiple passes of progressively higher detail. In Screens, the result is a much more responsive interface."* **ZRLE, Hextile, CopyRect, and RRE are never mentioned** in Edovia's docs — not found. — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/features/images-quality
- **Adaptive Quality is Mac-target-only** — *"only available when connecting to a Mac"* (inferred: it depends on Apple's Screen Sharing server extensions, not generic RFB, which is why it doesn't work against Windows/Linux VNC servers). — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/features/images-quality
- **"Compression" in Screens is actually display scaling, not an encoding.** The server sends a 50%-scaled desktop: *"The result will be an image with the same ratios but 50% smaller, which improves transfer speed and rendering"*, *"particularly useful when your Mac has several displays attached or when a 5K iMac is part of your scenario."* — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/features/images-quality
- Bandwidth caveat, verbatim: *"higher frame-rates and resolutions equal more data to push across the network, so these factors may affect display performance in Screens."* Video works (*"Video data is transferred to Screens as part of the remote display"*); audio never does. — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/faq/audio-video

## 8. Stated reasons for design choices — **thin, and notably evasive on architecture**

- **Edovia has published no architectural rationale.** There is no blog post, whitepaper, or doc explaining *why* UPnP/NAT-PMP rather than a relay, or *why* VNC rather than a custom protocol. I checked the full blog index (10 posts back to June 2025) and the complete doc trees. **Not found.** — https://blog.edovia.com/en/
- **The only stated reason for the biggest recent change is UX, not networking.** "Remote Access Is Now Built Into Screens for Mac" (June 22, 2026), verbatim: *"By integrating remote access directly into Screens, setup becomes simpler and managing your Mac is more straightforward."* And: *"New users can get started in just a few steps, while existing users can continue using the standalone version if they prefer."* The post gives **no technical detail** on port mapping, relay, or infrastructure. — https://blog.edovia.com/en/remote-access-now-built-into-screens
- **"Improving Screens Connect" (July 21, 2026) is the closest thing to an admission of trouble**, verbatim: *"We're currently making significant improvements to the Screens Connect service to improve reliability, compatibility, and performance."* It names the three exact axes where a port-mapping architecture is weakest — and still **discloses no mechanism**. (The fetched summary cited "Screens Connect 5.3.3 or later" and "Screens 5.10 or later" as versions to update to; the 5.10 figure conflicts with the release-notes page, which tops out at 5.8.12 — **treat that version number as unreliable**.) — https://blog.edovia.com/en/improving-screens-connect
- **The stated reason for recommending Tailscale is capitulation on traversal**, verbatim: *"Screens Connect is the easiest way to access your computer from outside your home or office. But if Screens Connect isn't available or doesn't work in your setup, there's another reliable option: Tailscale"* — appealing because Tailscale creates *"a secure VPN that creates a private network between your devices"* without requiring users to manually configure routers or firewalls. — https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/connecting-anywhere/tailscale
- **The stated reason for VNC is implicitly compatibility** — one client reaches Mac, Windows, Linux, and Raspberry Pi, each with its own "Preparing your…" guide. The cost is stated as protocol limitations they cannot fix (*"a limitation of the RFB protocol the app is using"*). — https://edovia.com/en/screens , https://help.edovia.com/en/screens-5/
- **Screens Connect's own docs concede the approach is fragile**, verbatim: *"Some manufacturers advertise their hardware as UPnP- or NAT-PMP-compatible, but some routers use buggy UPnP implementations."* And on failure: *"Try restarting your router and computer. If this doesn't seem to help, it's possible that your router doesn't support UPnP."* — https://edovia-1.gitbook.io/screens-connect-for-macos/troubleshooting/port-mapping-issues

## Bottom line for the design report

Screens is a **conventional RFB/VNC client over TCP** (5900, plus 22 for optional SSH tunneling), and its remote-access story is **UPnP/NAT-PMP/PCP inbound port mapping plus a dynamic-DNS-style directory lookup** against Edovia's server. There is **no relay, no STUN/ICE, no hole punching, and no session migration** — as of September 2026, after 15 years of the product. When the router won't grant a mapping (CGNAT, double NAT, corporate/managed networks, buggy UPnP), Edovia's own documented answer is **manual port forwarding or install Tailscale**. The marketing phrase *"strong, end-to-end encryption"* is materially stronger than the support documentation, which says only that Screens *"can"* connect through SSH tunnels — encryption is an opt-in toggle, not a property of the default path.
