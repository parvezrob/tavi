1 · Summary

On the morning of 9 September the owner was working in a terminal on the MacBook Air from the phone. At 07:00 the app showed "Reconnecting", then "MacBook Air isn't answering", then a blank screen. Force-quitting twice did nothing. Wi‑Fi and cellular behaved the same. At 07:47 the owner turned Tailscale off and on in the phone's VPN settings and it came back instantly. Nothing in Tavi or on the Mac had changed.

The cause is a known, open bug class in Tailscale's iPhone client: a socket swap during a network change kills its packet-reading loop and nothing restarts it. Tavi had exactly one route to the computer, and it ran through that loop. This paper records what was found, what comparable products do, and proposes an architecture with two independent routes to every host, end-to-end encryption that no relay can read, and a session layer that turns a dropped route into a stutter instead of a blank screen.

2 · The four minutes

Everything below is from the phone's own log archive (read over the cable with log collect --device), the Mac's tailscaled.log, and the host log. The app's own reason codes were not in the archive because they are logged at .info, which iOS keeps in memory only; Apple's network-layer lines were the substitute.

Three things were ruled out with evidence, so nobody chases them again: the second computer being offline (the owner had shut it down, unrelated), the Mac's load and its Xcode debug daemons (busy, not causal, and the failure recurred on LTE), and the phone's memory (the Tailscale extension stayed a single process at 27–33 MB of its 50 MB cap and was never killed). What remained was a process that was alive and no longer reading packets.

3 · Why it is structural, not bad luck

The research found the same signature reported repeatedly against Tailscale's iOS client, with the reporters doing exactly what Tavi does: interactive terminals from a phone.

IssueOpenedStateWhat it says
tailscale#195042026‑04‑23open"MagicSock ReceiveIPv4 goroutine stops, data plane dies while control plane stays up." Wi‑Fi and cellular "same result on both." A third-party root-cause post (2026‑08‑21): the receive closure gets ENOTCONN during a socket rebind, WireGuard treats it as fatal, nothing restarts the loop. Reporters: SSH/tmux "every 2–3 minutes"; Belay Mobile: "an interactive SSH terminal reliably exposes it; toggling Tailscale restores connectivity immediately."
tailscale#152712025‑03‑10open"iOS MagicSock fails. UDP/DERP fails." Status when failing: UDP = No, the same udp=false in our phone log. "Toggling TS on/off will typically but not always reestablish the connection."
tailscale#188892026‑03‑05openConnectivity fails shortly after connecting on iOS 26.4; toggling helps briefly.
tailscale#157762025‑04‑23open · P1Clients do not promptly notice losing their DERP relay; keepalives are one-way. This is the likeliest reason the relay did not carry the session either.

Two facts make it Tavi's problem rather than Tailscale's. First, no public iOS API lets one app observe or restart another vendor's VPN extension; Tavi can only see that the internet works and the computer does not. Second, App Store Review Guideline 4.2.3(i) says an app "should work on its own without requiring installation of another app to function." A Tailscale-only transport is a review risk on top of a reliability one.

Why it feels sudden, then persistent
The trigger is a network transition, not time. Hours of perfect use, then one socket swap lands in the wrong window and the reading loop dies. Force-quitting Tavi cannot help because the dead loop lives in Tailscale's process. Only recreating the VPN recreates the loop.

4 · Today's architecture

Tavi today is a Swift app talking to a small Node host over two WebSockets (events and terminal) and a set of HTTP routes. The only way from the phone to the host is the tailnet: the phone's Tailscale VPN extension, WireGuard or a DERP relay, the computer's tailscaled, and tailscale serve, which terminates TLS and forwards plain HTTP to the host on loopback.

What already works well and is kept: the terminal protocol resumes from a byte offset inside a 1 MiB ring the host keeps for two minutes after a drop, with a full repaint from herdr when the ring is missed; the events stream sends full snapshots so it is self-resuming; the phone's WebSockets run on Network.framework with a path monitor and an honest "Offline" verdict; pairing mints a per-device credential that the host can revoke within two seconds.

Six patterns carry the weight of the argument:

  • Every coding-agent product uses one outbound connection to a vendor relay and sells "no inbound ports" as the security feature. None hole-punches. Remote-desktop products punch because video needs UDP throughput; a terminal at 1–5 KB/s does not.
  • The products with the worst "works at home, fails remotely" reputations are the ones with no relay (Screens, Moonlight, Parsec on carrier-grade NAT). RustDesk merged WebRTC this week because "ICE covers a strictly larger connectivity set."
  • Roaming without re-authentication exists in four designs: Mosh, WireGuard, QUIC, and Chrome Remote Desktop's ICE restart. Plain WebSocket products drop on handover and re-dial.
  • Heartbeats converge on 3–5 seconds (Mosh 3, Tailscale 3, Eternal Terminal 5), because UDP NAT mappings expire around 30 seconds and a dead link must be noticed fast. Tavi's events heartbeat is 15 s × 2.
  • The outages customers experience as "your machine is offline" are control-plane outages: Jump Desktop's 24-hour message-bus partition, Happy's ~20-hour relay degradation, Tailscale Funnel dying silently this month while reporting healthy.
  • On iOS, every terminal app accepts that the process is suspended 20–30 s after backgrounding. Blink serialises mosh's state and rehydrates it on return. No transport changes this; a VPN keeps the tunnel up, not the app's socket.

6 · What "A+" has to mean

No app can promise 99.99 % of wall-clock time; the phone's radio and ISP are outside every SLA on earth (Cloudflare's, AWS's and Ably's all exclude them). What Tavi can promise is behaviour under a stated condition, measured as two different things, because "attempts that succeed" and "minutes a live session is broken" have different denominators.

IndicatorDefinitionTarget
Attempt successOpening the app or a terminal ends with an authenticated connection and the current state rendered within 5 s. A socket alone does not count.≥ 99.9 % at launch → ≥ 99.99 %
Session interruptionA foreground session is available while the last authenticated liveness proof is under 10 s old and the screen is current. Interruption runs from the last good observation to the first current screen after a fault. Heartbeat spacing never counts; a heartbeat answered while the screen is broken is not availability.99.9 % (≈ 6 min / 100 h) at launch → 99.99 % (36 s / 100 h)

Recovery after detectionWarm alternate route: about three round trips plus render. New connection on the surviving interface: about five.≈ 0.5 s home · ≈ 1 s intercontinental · ≤ 3 s cold

The condition: the phone has working internet to at least one relay provider, and the host process is up with a working uplink. The measurement: opt-in client counters and the chaos harness from #111, per release, over the first thirty days of that release's fleet. Until there is a fleet, four nines is a design target, not a marketing claim.

A single vendor cannot carry the target
Cloudflare had a ~6-hour global outage on 18 November 2025 and a 6-hour partial one on 20 February 2026. Fly.io had a 7-hour global outage in October 2024. Any one of those alone blows a year's four-nines budget. Two independently operated routes to every host, and a session that survives moving between them, is the whole design.

8 · Proposal A · a dumb relay with end-to-end TLS inside it

The app's whole surface is HTTP and WebSockets. If the relay is a dumb byte pipe and the host itself terminates TLS 1.3 with a key the phone pinned at pairing, then everything the app already does works unchanged through the relay: every API route, both WebSockets, uploads, the preview page. The bearer credential never leaves the encrypted channel. Revocation keeps working exactly as today. The relay operator sees ciphertext and connection metadata, nothing else. This is the model of Tailscale Funnel, the only hosted design in the survey where the provider cannot read the traffic.

What the phone does on a fault

Every liveness check is an authenticated challenge inside TLS, never an outer WebSocket ping: Cloudflare answers those itself without touching the host, so a pong proves only that the relay is alive. A challenge goes out every 5 s with a 5 s deadline; a route is dead when 10 s have passed since its last authenticated response. Path events come from the outer transport, because the loopback leg always looks healthy.

Identity, so the relay can be dumb

  • Host key pair. The host generates a key, serves TLS with a self-signed certificate for it, and the fingerprint shown on both screens is derived from it. Pairing v2 puts the full public key, both relays' endpoints and the single-use secret in the QR.
  • Device keys. The phone generates its own key pair at pairing; the host records the public key beside the bearer credential and publishes the list of its paired devices' public keys with every relay registration.
  • Admission without a deadlock. A returning phone signs the relay's challenge with its device key; nothing expires, nothing renews. First pairing uses a separate admission token registered by tavi pair for five minutes; the pairing secret itself is only ever shown to the host inside pinned TLS and is bound to the phone's device key, so a malicious relay cannot redeem it.
  • Revocation. The host's registry stays the single authority and needs no relay cooperation; the relay learns the updated list on the next registration update, stated as a delay, not "immediately". A wrong host key on one route quarantines that route only; the pin is never replaced automatically.

9 · Proposal B · iroh, one library that already does all of it

iroh is a Rust networking library, 1.0 since June 2026, that dials peers by public key, hole-punches with relay-coordinated discovery, and falls back to relays it runs or you run. Since version 0.96 a single QUIC connection keeps the relay path and the direct path at the same time; when the phone moves from Wi‑Fi to LTE the relay path carries traffic while the direct path is re-punched, with no new handshake. It ships a Swift xcframework and a Node binding, and needs no VPN extension. On paper it is the Parsec-and-Tailscale design in one dependency.

How the choice is made
Phase 1 is a one-week bake-off, not a gate that prejudges the answer. Both candidates are built to the same tiny workload (pair, host info, a terminal, a preview page with its hot-reload socket, one upload) and run through the same failure matrix: connect success, recovery tails on route loss and on interface loss, suspension and return, CPU, memory, battery, terminal latency during a maximum upload, and the Dhaka‑LTE→Virginia and Europe→Virginia geographies. It ends with numbers and an explicit selection. If iroh wins it becomes the principal transport and the relay in Proposal A shrinks to iroh's own relay; if the pipe wins, iroh may still return later as a direct-path route. Everything above the transport stays the same either way.

10 · The session layer, which is what the user actually feels

Routes decide whether bytes can flow. The session layer decides whether the person notices. It sits above whichever transport wins, and it is where most of the review effort went.

Three promises the review made the plan sharpen

  • "Exactly-once input" became "ordered and duplicate-free within one live session." Writing to a PTY and recording the acknowledgement are not one atomic act; a host that dies between them cannot later prove what happened. So when the ledger is gone the app says "delivery uncertain" and asks, which is what it does today. Expiry never turns uncertainty into a verdict.
  • "Never blank" became exact. A stale frame with content is never replaced by an empty live surface unless a repaint has delivered content or the user typed. The repaint boundary is a named heuristic (content after the last clear-screen, then 300 ms of quiet), because a clear-screen followed by a pause is exactly how a real repaint starts.
  • "Sub-second recovery" was tied to round trips and separated from the ten-second detection bound for silent stalls. A warm alternate removes establishment time, not detection time.

Predictive local echo, Mosh's trick of drawing a typed character before the host confirms it, stays an experiment for the last phase. The phone's existing composer already gives immediate feedback for typed text; coding-agent TUIs redraw arbitrarily; and Mosh's 70 % figure is Mosh's workload, not Tavi's.

12 · Cost and operations

Bandwidth is not the cost. At the modelled workload (2,000 concurrent sessions, two hours a day, roughly 10,000 users) the relay itself is cheap on either provider; per-message managed services would be one to two orders of magnitude worse, and ngrok's terms forbid redistributing its agent to customers at all.

Where the relay runsCompute / monthBandwidth / monthNotes
Cloudflare Workers + Durable Objects≈ $39$0no egress charge; incoming WebSocket messages billed at 20:1; if hibernation is defeated by our socket handling, duration alone could reach ≈ $700
VPS relays, three regions (Hetzner / Fly)$30–100$50–2,300egress varies 6× by region on Fly; India and Africa are the expensive ones; Hetzner includes generous egress
Managed realtime (Ably, AWS API Gateway WebSocket, PubNub)$550–9,000includedper-message pricing punishes keystroke traffic; not considered further

The real cost is people. From the first managed release, someone is on call; both providers are probed end to end from outside; certificate expiry and billing have alarms; rollouts are provider by provider with the other verified healthy first; VPS relays drain before restart; there is a runbook and a reconnect-storm test. None of that waits for ten thousand users.

The relay sees metadata: host identity, device public keys, phone and host addresses, timing, volume. Not content. The honest sentence for the app and the privacy policy is "Session content is encrypted between your phone and your computer. Relay operators can observe connection metadata." A no-managed-relay mode (direct, Tailscale, or a self-hosted relay) keeps the PRD's "no mandatory hosted relay" promise as a choice; the default changes.

14 · The review record

Each memo version was sent to GPT‑6 Astra seats that had not seen the project, with the research files, the incident report and the repository sources, under the repo's second-opinion process. No round approved. Every finding is dispositioned in the plan's replay tables.

What the reviews changed, in one line each

  • A Noise-protocol design was replaced by inner TLS, because the fingerprint was never a public key and the relay would otherwise have had to carry every HTTP surface itself.
  • Route racing was split from terminal ownership: as written, a slower route would have evicted the winner under today's "last attachment wins" rule.
  • "Exactly-once input" became honest about lost state.
  • The one-second recovery promise was tied to round trips and separated from the ten-second detection bound.
  • "No control plane" became "coordination written down": registrations with generations, endpoints cached with addresses, two DNS operators.
  • Costs were labelled as estimates with the $700 downside stated; App Review 4.2.7 was marked unresolved; the PRD reversal was named as the owner's decision.
  • The first-pairing admission was redesigned so the pairing secret is shown only to the host.

15 · Decisions for the owner

  1. The reversal, with its running cost.

    Default route becomes a Tavi-operated relay, end-to-end encrypted, with a no-managed-relay mode kept. Saying yes means Tavi runs infrastructure from the first release, an estimated $40–800 a month at 10,000 users depending on what phase 1 measures, plus a named person on call.

  2. The launch bargain.

    Ship the first relay release at the end of phase 4 at 99.9 % session availability with two stated limitations: input typed across a route change is reported as uncertain rather than replayed, and resuming after suspension starts from a fresh repaint. 99.99 % is the target the next phases work toward. Or hold the launch until 99.99 % is demonstrated, which needs a fleet to measure on.

  3. Approve the bake-off week.

    Phase 1 spends one week building both transport candidates to the same workload and choosing with numbers rather than by argument.

  4. Which customer regions matter first.

    Bangladesh and one of Europe or the US is the assumption; relay locations follow from where customers and their hosts are.

  5. Tailscale's place.

    Keep an optional "Tailscale mode" that pairing no longer requires, so existing pairs keep working through migration; or drop Tailscale once the relay ships and have existing users re-pair. Support cost is the difference.
